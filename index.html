Sand Zen Studio works offline. The game does not send your gardens or personal information to the developer or third parties. It does not require an account and contains no advertising.

1. Information collection and sharing

The game does not collect or transmit personal information, device identifiers, location, usage analytics, or crash reports to the developer. It does not include advertising or enabled analytics services. We do not sell or share information from the game.

2. Information stored on your device

The game stores information locally to support gameplay:

  • Saved garden sand surfaces, garden names, creation dates, sand selections, and preview images.
  • Game preferences, including graphics quality and interaction settings.

This information is stored in the game's application storage. It is used to restore your gardens and preferences. The developer does not have remote access to it. Please avoid including personal or sensitive information in garden names.

3. Retention and deletion

Saved gardens and preferences remain on your device until you remove them. You can delete individual saved gardens from the game's saved-gardens gallery. Resetting the active sand tray does not delete previously saved gardens.

To remove all local game data, use Android Settings to clear the game's app storage. Uninstalling the game ordinarily removes its local app data. Device backups may retain copies or restore data after reinstallation; backup retention and deletion depend on your operating system, device settings, and backup provider. The game does not operate a cloud-save service.

4. Permissions and security

The game uses vibration for optional haptic feedback. It does not request camera, microphone, contacts, precise location, or broad access to your files, and this release has no Android Internet permission.

Local files are protected by the access controls provided by your operating system. Protecting your device and managing its backups remain important because local files are not guaranteed to be inaccessible on a compromised device.
